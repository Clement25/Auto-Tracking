## Analysis & Outlook

**Sector Recovery from July Selloff Enters Maturation Phase**
The semiconductor sector's dramatic July 2026 correction — the SOX index's worst month in 18 years — proved transient, as the AI demand narrative reasserted itself through a robust August earnings season led by NVIDIA's blowout quarter. As we head into the Labor Day holiday weekend, the sector has largely retraced those losses with the SOX ETF still up roughly 86% for the year. The composition of leadership has shifted meaningfully, however: memory (MU), foundry infrastructure (TSM), and custom silicon (MRVL) have emerged as the new momentum leaders, while some of the more richly-valued design software names (CDNS) and legacy analog plays (TXN) face relative underperformance headwinds.

**The Broadcom Margin Question: Signal or Noise?**
The most important data point from the week came from Broadcom's Q3 FY2026 earnings, which illustrate the central tension in the AI chip investment thesis. AVGO's 221% AI revenue surge and $58B full-year AI revenue guide are genuinely extraordinary — but gross margin compression from ~78% to a guided 73% has raised the question of whether AI semiconductor economics are structurally deteriorating as custom silicon and HBM content grows. Investors bifurcated their reaction: AVGO sold off 6% immediately post-earnings but partially recovered, while MRVL — facing the same HBM/XPU margin dynamics — rallied 7% by Friday on the thesis that MRVL is still earlier in its AI ramp and thus hasn't yet priced in the margin normalization. This margin debate will be the defining narrative for the sector through the remainder of 2026.

**Intel's Transformation Is Being Priced In**
INTC's 222% YTD gain makes it the sector's most dramatic outperformer — and the most contested. Lip-Bu Tan has successfully reset market expectations for Intel Foundry Services, secured credible customer commitments from Apple, Microsoft, and SpaceX, and demonstrated improved execution discipline. Yet the foundry business is still deeply unprofitable and cash-consumptive, and the path to achieving manufacturing parity with TSMC remains multi-year. INTC's current ~$95 price implies substantial foundry success is being discounted; any stumble in execution could see violent mean reversion. Bulls argue the optionality is unique at scale; bears see a "still-burning-cash-but-now-more-expensive" story.

**Memory Supercycle: DRAM and NAND Scarcity Extends Visibility**
Micron's crossing of the $1,000/share threshold is a symbolic milestone but reflects real fundamental strength. The structural mismatch between AI-driven HBM demand growth and new capacity coming online (which takes 18–24 months to ramp) creates unusual pricing visibility. With Micron reporting September 30, Samsung reporting shortly after, and SK Hynix's earnings following, the memory earnings corridor will be closely watched for any signs of demand pull-forward or customer inventory build — the primary risks to the memory supercycle thesis. For now, the setup remains favorable.

**Equipment Stocks: Best-in-Class Positioning Into 2027**
Applied Materials, KLA, Lam Research, and ASML all remain positioned as essential picks-and-shovels plays on a structural semiconductor capex cycle projected to grow at high single digits YoY through 2027. With Intel reinvesting aggressively in U.S. fabs, TSMC building out Arizona capacity, and Samsung expanding Korea and Texas facilities, equipment order books remain strong. ASML's EUV monopoly is irreplaceable on a 5–7 year horizon; AMAT's diversified tool portfolio provides exposure across etch, deposition, and CMP processes. Jefferies analyst Blayne Curtis specifically highlighted KLA alongside NVDA and AVGO as among his top 2026 picks.

**Risks to Monitor**
Key risks heading into the fall 2026 earnings season include: (1) AI capex fatigue — if hyperscalers see ROI delays on AI investments, spending could moderate quickly; (2) geopolitical risk around Taiwan and U.S. export controls, which could disrupt TSMC, ASML, and AMAT supply chains; (3) the Broadcom margin compression precedent spreading to peers; and (4) a potential valuation reset if the AI revenue ramp takes longer than modeled. Micron's September 30 earnings report will be the next major sector catalyst.
