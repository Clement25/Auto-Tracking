## Analysis & Outlook

**A Week Defined by Leadership Shock.** The resignation of Jensen Huang as NVIDIA's CEO on November 12 was the most consequential single event in US semiconductor markets this year. Huang was not merely NVIDIA's leader — he was the embodiment of the AI chip narrative that propelled the sector to multi-trillion-dollar valuations. His abrupt departure amid a scandal with potential national security dimensions triggered a re-rating of NVIDIA and a sympathy sell-off across AI-exposed chip names. With NVDA now trading near 8-month lows at $162.85, the stock has shed roughly 23-25% from its June 2026 peak of ~$236, compressing an estimated $1+ trillion in market value. The key near-term catalyst: NVIDIA's Q3 FY2027 earnings on November 17 under Interim CEO Colette Kress, who must simultaneously stabilize a $3.97T company, navigate a congressional investigation into potential export control violations, and manage relationships with hyperscaler customers reconsidering their NVIDIA dependence.

**AMD's Sympathy Sell-Off Masks Improving Fundamentals.** AMD's -8.75% decline on Friday is the week's sharpest single-day move in the index, but it comes against a backdrop of genuine analyst optimism. Two separate firms — KeyBanc and Raymond James — issued upgrades or Buy ratings this week with the Raymond James target of $641 implying 20% upside from current levels. AMD's AI accelerator business (MI300X/MI350 series) has been capturing share from NVIDIA in hyperscaler deployments, and the company remains a primary beneficiary of the structural diversification trend away from single-vendor GPU dependence. Friday's selloff looks more like high-beta risk-off than a fundamental deterioration.

**Equipment Names: Volatility Around Earnings.** The semiconductor equipment trio — AMAT, LRCX, and KLAC — staged a notable midweek recovery with gains of 8-11% on November 9-11 ahead of Applied Materials' earnings. AMAT's subsequent pullback to $370.50 (from a $396 intraday high on Nov 11) reflects post-earnings mean reversion and broader sector pressure, not a deterioration in equipment fundamentals. LRCX posted record revenue of $5.84 billion in its latest quarter, reinforcing that front-end semiconductor investment remains robust despite near-term demand concerns. ASML continues to hold near its 52-week highs (~$1,495 vs. $1,653 high) — a sign that advanced lithography demand, particularly EUV for N2 and 1.4nm node buildouts at TSMC and Samsung, remains structurally sound.

**TSMC as the Sector's Anchor.** TSM's relative outperformance (-1.67% vs. sector average of -3.5%+) reflects its unique position: the foundry is both insulated from and central to the AI hardware buildout. TSMC manufactures chips for both NVIDIA and AMD, benefits from their rivalry, and its Q3 growth data strongly undercuts the bear case that AI capex is decelerating. TSMC's $2.07T estimated market cap and disciplined capital allocation make it the most defensible name in the sector should a prolonged risk-off environment persist. The Motley Fool's November 13 piece arguing that TSMC's results prove the sell-off is "overdone" resonates as a contrarian catalyst heading into next week.

**Risk Factors & Forward Look.** The most significant near-term risks are: (1) NVIDIA's earnings report on November 17 — a guidance miss or negative update from Kress could extend NVDA's decline and amplify sector pressure; (2) escalating national security scrutiny of Huang-era chip export practices, which could trigger further regulatory action; (3) the structural shift by cloud hyperscalers toward custom ASICs, which is a multi-year headwind for merchant silicon vendors; and (4) the Marvell-Lumentum deal overhang, which creates near-term uncertainty for MRVL shareholders. On the upside, the universal sector decline creates a potential mean-reversion opportunity — historically, broad-based chip selloffs of this magnitude have resolved higher within 4-8 weeks when macroeconomic fundamentals remain intact. Investors should watch NVDA's November 17 earnings as the pivotal inflection point for sector direction heading into year-end.
