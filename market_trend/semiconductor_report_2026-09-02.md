## Analysis & Outlook

**Session Narrative: Macro vs. AI Fundamentals**
September 2, 2026 saw the semiconductor sector caught between two powerful forces: a deteriorating macro backdrop (rising bond yields, elevated oil) and an exceptionally strong underlying AI demand cycle. The 10-year Treasury at 4.8% and 30-year at a 20-year high of 5.31% triggered a valuation reset for growth stocks broadly, but stocks with the clearest AI earnings catalysts (NVDA, ASML) managed to hold or post modest gains while more macro-sensitive or pre-earnings names came under pressure.

**Broadcom: The Sector's Near-Term Pivot Point**
Broadcom's post-close Q3 earnings report is the most significant near-term catalyst for the entire sector. With consensus expecting 84% revenue growth and AI semiconductor revenue growing over 200% YoY, the question is whether AVGO can beat expectations and provide guidance that extends the AI capex cycle narrative into CY2027. A strong beat-and-raise would likely trigger a broad sector recovery; a miss or cautious guidance could extend selling pressure, particularly in AI-adjacent names. AVGO's stock is down just 0.18% ahead of the report, suggesting options traders are pricing a contained reaction range.

**Equipment Names: Correction or Structural Bear?**
The semiconductor equipment group (AMAT -3.61%, LRCX -3.74%) has been the sector's biggest underperformer over the past several weeks, with the group off roughly 20-30% from recent peaks. Cantor Fitzgerald's upgrade suggests this is a cyclical correction rather than a structural break—Intel's $20B capex commitment and continued TSM capacity expansion support a multiyear equipment spending cycle. However, the rate environment remains a risk: if the 10-year sustains above 5%, the valuation headwind on capex-intensive businesses becomes increasingly difficult to overcome in the near term.

**AI Memory: Micron's HBM Dominance**
Micron Technology's 220% YTD gain reflects a genuine paradigm shift in memory demand. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI GPUs is structurally undersupplied through at least 2027, and Micron is one of only three companies capable of producing it (alongside SK Hynix and Samsung). The company's 346% YoY revenue growth speaks to the severity of the demand-supply imbalance. With 27 of 30 analysts bullish and September 30 earnings approaching, MU remains the sector's clearest fundamental story—though its elevated valuation (~$1T market cap) now prices in much of the good news.

**Stocks to Watch**
- **NVDA**: Near-term support at $216 (Sept 1 low); a close above $228 would signal resumption of the prior uptrend. Key metric: AVGO earnings guidance for AI silicon demand.
- **AVGO**: Post-close earnings today—if AI revenue guidance exceeds the implied $19–20B for Q4 fiscal 2026, expect a significant gap higher.
- **INTC**: Despite today's minor recovery, Intel's 143% YTD gain makes it the most exposed name to rates-driven multiple compression. Technical support at $86.
- **AMAT / LRCX**: Cantor's upgrade provides a fundamental floor, but momentum remains negative. A reversal in the 10-year yield below 4.5% would be the primary catalyst for a sharp equipment group recovery.
- **MU**: September 30 earnings are the next major catalyst. HBM supply tightness and AI infrastructure spending trajectories remain the key debates.

**Risks**
The key risk to the sector remains macro: sustained yields above 5% could compress multiples across the board, with highest-PE names (NVDA, MPWR, SNPS, CDNS) most exposed. Geopolitical risks (Strait of Hormuz closure, U.S.-China tensions around TSM and ASML export controls) add a second layer of tail risk. On the upside, any resolution to the oil/yield shock or a strong Broadcom guidance beat could catalyze a rapid sector rotation back into semiconductors from defensive positions.
