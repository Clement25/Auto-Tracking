## Top Movers

### Biggest Decliners

**1. AMD — Advanced Micro Devices (-8.75%, -$51.44)**
AMD posted the steepest single-day decline among tracked names on Friday. Despite solid underlying fundamentals — Q3 results beat expectations and management guided AI chip revenue toward ~$58B for 2026 (up 190% YoY) — AMD was caught in the broader AI-chip sell-off triggered by NVIDIA's CEO transition. Early-week rotation from NVDA into AMD reversed sharply, with investors indiscriminately selling the sector. KeyBanc upgraded AMD, and William Blair raised its rating to Outperform, citing Instinct GPU share gains.

**2. AVGO — Broadcom Inc. (-4.76%, -$17.35)**
Broadcom fell $17.35 from its prior $364.36 close despite being widely viewed as the primary near-term beneficiary of NVIDIA's uncertainty. Goldman Sachs added AVGO to its top picks list; JPMorgan reiterated an Overweight rating. **3. MRVL — Marvell Technology (-4.68%, -$7.70)**

### Most Resilient (Least Decline)

**1. ASML — ASML Holding (-0.13%, -$1.96)**
ASML was essentially flat, the most resilient name in the group. As the sole provider of EUV lithography equipment globally, ASML operates in a category of one, insulating it from near-term U.S. AI sentiment shifts. Its business is underpinned by TSMC and Samsung capex plans.

**2. MCHP — Microchip Technology (-0.64%, -$0.40)**
Microchip Technology eked out a near-flat session, a relative outperformer given its focus on microcontrollers and mixed-signal semiconductors for industrial and automotive markets — segments less directly tied to AI data center spending. **3. TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor (-1.67%, -$6.75)**
TSMC held up relatively well as NVIDIA's CEO uncertainty underscores long-term demand rather than a structural change in AI compute requirements. TSMC remains the foundry of record for NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, and Apple, giving it diversified exposure across the AI-compute chain.

## Analysis & Outlook

**A Leadership Shock at the Sector's Bellwether**

The defining event of the week was Jensen Huang's resignation from NVIDIA on November 12 — the single most consequential leadership change in the semiconductor industry in decades. Huang, who founded NVIDIA in 1993 and personally architected the company's pivot to AI computing, leaves behind an organization generating north of $60 billion in quarterly revenue and holding a ~70% share of the AI accelerator market. The market reaction — a loss of over $1 trillion in NVIDIA's market value over the period — is proportional to the perceived key-person risk. While NVIDIA's products, partnerships, and software ecosystem (CUDA) are institutional and self-sustaining, there is no consensus replacement for Huang's combination of technical vision, long-term strategic thinking, and customer relationships. CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO provides stability but not equivalent visionary leadership. The board's deliberate CEO search, with an opening left for Huang's potential return per major investors, signals that Wall Street and insiders alike see his absence as the primary risk.

**Sector Rotation and Collateral Damage**

The near-term beneficiaries of NVIDIA's uncertainty — Broadcom and AMD — initially saw rotation inflows before selling pressure overwhelmed the trade. Broadcom, with its custom AI ASIC (XPU) business and high-speed networking dominance, is structurally positioned as a primary alternative to NVIDIA for hyperscale customers building proprietary AI silicon. AMD's Instinct GPU roadmap represents the most credible GPU alternative. However, AMD's 8.75% decline on Friday suggests that even the beneficiaries are not immune when sentiment-driven selling accelerates. The key question for the week ahead: does NVIDIA's CEO uncertainty accelerate order diversification toward AVGO and AMD, or does the entire AI-infrastructure spending decision cycle pause pending clarity on NVIDIA's strategic direction?

**Equipment Makers Facing a Double Squeeze**

The semiconductor equipment sub-sector (AMAT, LRCX, KLAC) is experiencing compounded pressure. Already down 17–26% over the prior month from their June peaks, these names face both cyclical headwinds (a choppy equipment capex cycle as fabs digest prior-year investments) and sentiment-driven selling alongside the broader sector. The irony is that if AI infrastructure spending remains on its projected $1.3T trajectory for 2027, the leading edge capex implied by that — TSMC's N2 and N2P ramps, Samsung's gate-all-around investment, Intel Foundry 18A — translates into sustained equipment demand. ASML's resilience (-0.13%) reflects this duality: the EUV monopoly insulates it from near-term sentiment while keeping it tightly coupled to the secular demand trend.

**AI Demand Intact; Near-Term Volatility Is a Sentiment Story**

The bull case for semiconductors — that AI training and inference workloads will drive exponential compute demand through 2030 — is unaffected by a CEO transition at NVIDIA. The five hyperscalers are locked into multi-year capex cycles, NVIDIA has shipped its Blackwell architecture, and AMD is ramping Instinct. Micron's HBM3E position is commercially mature. What has changed is the risk premium attached to NVIDIA itself, and by contagion, the entire sector. Goldman Sachs, with an oversold designation on NVDA, AVGO, and AMD, is essentially calling the sell-off a sentiment event rather than a fundamental one. The case for a snapback is clear if the board announces a credible CEO or Huang's return materializes.

**Stocks to Watch in the Week Ahead**

- **NVDA:** Any CEO search update — rumors of internal/external candidates or Huang's potential reinstatement — will dominate price action. Watch for the $148.80 52-week low as a potential support test.
- **AVGO:** Broadcom's custom ASIC wins — especially if any hyperscaler publicly references accelerating orders — could trigger a significant rebound. The stock is pricing in uncertainty at $347 vs. its $495 52-week high.
- **AMD:** AMD's earnings report and Instinct shipment data will be the next major fundamental catalyst. The -8.75% move likely overshoots fundamentals.
- **MU:** Micron remains the cheapest large-cap name on PEG in the group. HBM4 demand visibility and any update on AI server memory pricing are key catalysts.
- **AMAT/LRCX/KLAC:** Equipment order books for Q4 2026 and 2027 guidance will determine whether the 20–26% drawdowns represent a buying opportunity or the front edge of a deeper capex correction.
