## Latest News & Analyst Commentary

### Sector-Wide

**"Second-Worst Day on Record" for Chip ETF — Thursday, November 5**
The iShares Semiconductor ETF and Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) suffered their second-worst single-day decline on record on Thursday, November 5. The SOX fell approximately 9–12% (sources diverge), erasing hundreds of billions in market value. The catalyst was the conclusion of the US midterm elections, which returned a Democratic-controlled Congress. Investors feared that new legislation could restrict AI data center construction, increase regulatory oversight of chip exports, and potentially curb hyperscaler capital expenditures.

**US Added 165K Jobs in October — Helping Friday's Rebound**
The October payrolls report (released Friday morning) showed 165,000 jobs added, slightly ahead of expectations. This alleviated recession concerns and supported a "risk-on" bounce across equities, with semiconductors—already deeply oversold—leading the recovery. The SOX recovered approximately +6.5% by midday Friday.

**Taiwan Curbs Short Selling on Semiconductor Stocks**
Taiwan's financial regulator moved to restrict short selling on domestic semiconductor stocks after TSMC (TSM) led the global chip selloff. TSM's US-listed ADRs had fallen 10.78% on Thursday to $353.10. The curb helped stabilize sentiment: TSM jumped +5.42% Friday to ~$372.25.

**AI Data Center Spending Remains Robust Despite Policy Risk**
Per KPMG and IDC forecasts, the global semiconductor market is expected to reach $1.29–$1.6 trillion in 2026, driven by AI infrastructure. Hyperscaler capex guidance for calendar 2026 is massive: Amazon (~$220B), Alphabet (~$195–205B), Microsoft (~$175B), Meta (~$130–145B). However, 30–50% of planned 2026 AI data center capacity may slip to 2028 due to power grid interconnection queues and construction bottlenecks.

## Analysis & Outlook

**A Historic Selloff and Its Aftermath**

Thursday, November 5, 2026 will be remembered as one of the worst days in the history of US semiconductor stocks. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell 9–12%—its second-worst single-session loss on record—erasing hundreds of billions in market value. The proximate trigger was the conclusion of the US midterm elections, which yielded a Democratic-controlled Congress. Markets feared that new legislation could restrict AI data center construction, impose export controls on advanced chips, or curtail hyperscaler investment in AI infrastructure. While these regulatory concerns may ultimately prove overstated, the market's reaction underscored just how richly valued the AI trade had become, and how much of the bull case depended on an unimpeded buildout of AI infrastructure.

**The Friday Bounce: Relief Rally or Sustainable Recovery?**

Friday's ~6.5% rebound in the SOX was substantial but incomplete. It followed a solid October jobs report (165K added) that relieved recession fears, combined with aggressive dip-buying from institutional investors. TSM's gain was amplified by Taiwan's short-selling curb. AMD's near-9% rise and Micron's +5% gain led the AI-exposed names higher. However, the bounce fails to erase Thursday's damage—and several stocks (QCOM, MCHP, TXN) are still nursing idiosyncratic wounds that the sector-wide rebound did not fully heal. The question investors must answer over the coming weeks is whether the Thursday selloff marked a durable top in the AI trade, or a capitulation washout before the next leg higher.

**Key Themes Driving the Sector**

Three themes dominate: (1) **AI demand**, which remains structurally robust but vulnerable to regulatory and macro shocks; (2) **company-specific earnings divergence**, with AI-infrastructure beneficiaries (NVDA, AMD, AMAT, ASML, TSM) outperforming analog/automotive-exposed names (TXN, QCOM, SWKS, MCHP) on a year-to-date basis despite the October–November correction; and (3) **policy risk**, which is now a first-order variable following the midterm results. Additionally, Marvell's $115B Lumentum acquisition introduced deal risk and strategic uncertainty around one of the sector's AI-custom-ASIC darlings, adding noise to an already volatile week.

**Stocks to Watch**

The two most critical near-term events are **NVIDIA's fiscal Q3 earnings on November 17** and **Applied Materials' results on November 12**. NVIDIA is the bellwether: if it delivers another beat-and-raise, with strong Blackwell GPU shipment guidance, the sector selloff will likely be viewed as a buying opportunity. If NVIDIA disappoints or provides cautious forward guidance—pointing to data center customers pausing capex—it could validate the bears and extend the correction. Applied Materials' WFE order commentary will offer a parallel read on 2027 semiconductor equipment demand. Meanwhile, Cadence and Synopsys stand out as relatively defensive AI plays: EDA software revenues are recurring and less tied to fab construction cycles, and their double-digit Friday gains suggest investors are rotating to quality.

**Risks and Forward-Looking Commentary**

The near-term risk set has expanded meaningfully. Regulatory risk now joins the existing headwinds of AI capex sustainability doubts, neocloud debt concerns, and China export control uncertainty. Memory stocks (MU) have fallen over 40% from their highs, pricing in a potential demand air pocket. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, even after Friday's bounce, sits dramatically below its October highs. With NVDA earnings 11 days away, volatility is likely to remain elevated. Longer-term, the structural case for semiconductors—hyperscaler AI spend projected at $700B+ combined for 2026, global semiconductor markets potentially exceeding $1.5T—remains intact. But the market's tolerance for uncertainty has clearly diminished, and any hint of regulatory action or demand-side weakness will likely be met with outsized selling. Investors with long horizons may find attractive entry points forming in names like TSM, AMD, and ASML; those with shorter time horizons should prepare for continued turbulence through Nvidia's November 17 earnings report.
