## Analysis & Outlook

**A Divergence Day: Custom Silicon Wins, Merchant GPU Story Wobbles**

August 20 was defined by a single headline: Marvell's expanded partnership with Google to build custom silicon across the TPU ecosystem. The stock's near-10% surge on a $12.2B warrant deal is not just a company-specific event — it is a strong signal about where hyperscaler dollars are moving. Google, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft are all accelerating custom-ASIC programs to reduce dependence on NVIDIA GPUs, and the incremental winners of that shift are the ASIC design partners (Marvell, Broadcom, Alchip) and the advanced-packaging supply chain (TSMC CoWoS, ASE). NVIDIA's modest decline into its Aug 26 print reflects this two-sided dynamic — the AI capex pie is still growing rapidly, but the composition is starting to fragment.

**Semi-Cap Equipment Still Under Pressure**

KLAC, LRCX, and AMAT all traded lower on the day, extending the previous week's underperformance. Equipment makers are the sector's most leveraged read-through to AI capex *plans* rather than current shipments — every hyperscaler capex reset or Wall Street Journal piece flagging off-balance-sheet AI commitments produces disproportionate volatility here. That said, the underlying capacity build is real: SEMI's forecast of 69% advanced-node capacity growth through 2028 is a multi-year tailwind, and any near-term weakness in the equipment names may prove to be a buying opportunity for investors with a two-year horizon. The key catalyst to watch is the sequence of TSMC N2 and Intel 18A ramp updates through year-end.

**EDA Names Need a Narrative Reset**

Cadence and Synopsys remain roughly 10% off their August highs on the Moonshot Kimi K3 chip-design headline. This is likely overdone — even if AI-native EDA becomes a real long-run threat, the design-tools moat is built on decades of PDK integration, foundry qualification, and IP libraries that a single 48-hour demo does not replicate. Synopsys' Aug 26 earnings print is the near-term catalyst that could either reinforce the disruption narrative (if bookings guide down) or dispel it (if AI *accelerates* customer tool spend rather than replacing it).

**Memory Remains the Highest-Conviction Trade**

Micron continues to look like the cleanest fundamental story in the sector. HBM allocations through 2026 are sold out, HBM4 is committed into 2027, and DRAM industry revenue is on pace to more than double YoY per multiple industry trackers. Historically, memory has been a boom-bust cyclical, but the current cycle — driven by structural AI demand and disciplined supply — has more of a "utility with pricing power" character than any Micron cycle since the mid-2010s. A path to sustained 80%+ HBM gross margins keeps MU on the short list of names that could justify meaningfully higher multiples even from here.

**Forward-Looking Setup**

The near-term calendar is dense: NVIDIA (Aug 26), Marvell (Aug 27), and Broadcom (early September) will collectively reset sector sentiment. Base case is that NVIDIA beats and raises modestly — enough to stabilize the tape but not enough to spark a re-rally to fresh highs given the demanding starting valuation. Marvell walks in with a fresh Google catalyst and elevated expectations; the bar is high after today's surge. Broadcom's XPU commentary — particularly around Google and Meta custom silicon — is now arguably the most-watched data point in the entire earnings cycle. Three names to watch into the next two weeks: **MRVL** (does today's move stick post-earnings?), **NVDA** (Blackwell/Rubin guidance signals sector direction), and **MU** (HBM pricing commentary is the highest-signal readout of AI compute demand health). Investors should expect elevated single-name dispersion — the sector is no longer trading as a monolith.
