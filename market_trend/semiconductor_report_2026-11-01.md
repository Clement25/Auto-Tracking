### Sector-Wide

**Intel's Q4 Shock Sets Off Contagion Selloff (Oct 30, 2026)**
Intel's Q3 2026 report delivered a double-shock: while EPS beat estimates ($0.52 vs. $0.41), revenue of $16.51B missed consensus by a thin margin, and the Q4 outlook was deeply disappointing. Management guided Q4 revenue to $15–16B (vs. $17.1B expected) and EPS of $0.25 (vs. $0.43 expected). CEO and CFO David Zinsner stated: *"We see the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year."* An Israeli factory shutdown adds ~$300M in Q4 revenue headwinds and ~200bps of gross margin pressure. The breadth of the selloff — with NVIDIA, AMD, MRVL, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, TSM, ASML all declining 4–11% — reflected market-wide reassessment of AI chip demand narratives, not just PC exposure.

**US Midterm Elections: Taiwan Chip Risk in Focus (Nov 3, 2026)**

Despite the near-term selloff, these structural tailwinds remain intact.

## Analysis & Outlook

**October 30 Selloff: Intel's Warning Shot and the Fragility of the AI Narrative**

The October 30, 2026 session will likely be remembered as one of the most consequential single-day sell-offs in semiconductor sector history. Intel's Q3 2026 earnings call — released after the close on October 29 — delivered a bombshell Q4 guidance miss that cascaded through every corner of the semiconductor ecosystem. INTC shares fell 28.58% to $65.76, the stock's largest one-day drop since 1974, wiping out a substantial portion of what had been a remarkable 2026 rally. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) declined approximately 7.2% — one of its steepest session losses in years.

**PC Weakness: A Known Unknown Becomes a Known**

Intel's Q4 guidance made explicit what many bulls had been hoping was a transitory issue: the PC replacement cycle is flagging, and management now expects this weakness to persist "at least through the first half of 2027." This read-across is deeply negative for AMD (Q3 earnings due Nov 3), Qualcomm (earnings Nov 4), and Microchip Technology (earnings Nov 5) — all of which will face investors freshly sensitized to demand disappointments. AMD's 52-week high of $658.52 — hit just weeks ago — now appears to have been peak euphoria pricing.

**AI Remains Structurally Intact, But Doubts Creep In**

The selloff must be contextualized against an extraordinary year for AI-driven chip demand. Micron is up ~281% year-to-date. NVIDIA's market cap of $4.93T represents an unparalleled concentration of tech value. Broadcom reported 221% year-over-year AI semiconductor revenue growth. Omdia projects overall semiconductor revenues will surge 94.1% in 2026 — making it potentially a $1+ trillion year for the industry. SK Hynix's signal of a memory supply shortage into 2030 suggests AI infrastructure buildout is far from over.

Yet Intel's results inject a critical nuance: the AI boom is far from uniform. While hyperscaler GPU demand (NVDA's domain) and custom silicon (AVGO, MRVL) remain robust, traditional compute categories — PC processors, client chips, standard logic — are struggling. Morgan Stanley's 10-15% further downside warning reflects concern that the market has been too broadly re-rating semiconductor stocks upward on AI optimism, without adequately distinguishing winners from losers.

**Election Uncertainty Adds a Geopolitical Dimension**

The November 3 midterm elections introduce a potential policy wildcard. Bloomberg has specifically flagged Taiwan Semiconductor as a name under potential pressure in certain electoral outcomes, given ongoing debates about Taiwan policy, CHIPS Act implementation, and export controls. TSM's -6.20% session loss already reflects some of this risk being priced in. ASML, the Dutch EUV monopolist, also faces export control risk if U.S.-allied restrictions on advanced chip equipment to China are tightened or loosened.

**Stocks to Watch Next Week**

- **AMD (reports Nov 3):** The PC warning from Intel is a direct read-across risk. However, AMD's data center GPU ramp and strong client share gains could offset concerns. The stock is down ~23% from its 52-week high — a failed beat on PC could send it lower; a strong data center beat could reverse the Intel-driven selloff.
- **QCOM (reports Nov 4):** Qualcomm's new Dragonwing enterprise chip and Snapdragon 8 Elite ramp are positives, but the PC/modem business may face scrutiny. The -11.12% pre-earnings decline creates a high-risk, high-reward setup.
- **MCHP (reports Nov 5):** Microchip has already been under pressure, with shares down from a 52-week high of $105.91 to $77.34. Industrial and automotive end-market commentary will be closely watched.
- **AMAT (reports Nov 12):** Applied Materials is down 47% from its 52-week high of $739.67. Strong demand from AI-driven fab buildout — particularly from TSMC's Arizona expansion — should provide a positive backdrop despite near-term sentiment weakness.

**Risks**

- Intel's Q4 weakness spreading to broader end-market demand expectations
- Midterm election outcomes affecting Taiwan chip policy and export controls
- Morgan Stanley's projection of an additional 10-15% drawdown in semiconductor stocks
- PC/consumer chip demand remaining soft through H1 2027
- Power grid constraints limiting the pace of AI data center construction

**Conclusion**

The semiconductor sector enters November 2026 at an inflection point. Structural AI demand remains compelling, but the Intel earnings shock has forced a painful reassessment of which companies are truly AI-driven and which are legacy-compute businesses benefiting from a rising tide. The next two weeks — with earnings from AMD, QCOM, MCHP, and AMAT — will be pivotal in determining whether October 30 was a buying opportunity or the beginning of a more protracted correction.
