# Top Papers — AI Agents & Embodied AI (2026-08-02)

## [Beyond the Leaderboard: A Synthesis of Tool-Use, Planning, and Reasoning Failures in Large Language Model Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05775)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2607.05775)
**TL;DR:** Synthesizes findings from 27 benchmark, taxonomy, and audit papers (2023–2026) spanning 19 distinct benchmarks to identify systematic failure modes in LLM agents across tool use, planning, and reasoning. The analysis reveals that agent failures cluster around tool-call hallucination, multi-step planning collapse, and brittle reasoning under uncertainty. Provides a unified taxonomy to guide future evaluation design beyond leaderboard rankings.
**Tags:** `agents`, `llm-agents`, `tool-use`, `planning`, `evaluation`, `benchmarks`

## [HAS-Bench: Evaluating LLM-Based Human-Agent Systems under Configurable Human Participation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04329)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2607.04329)
**TL;DR:** Introduces HAS-Bench, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate human-agent collaboration by varying the degree of human participation in the loop. Demonstrates that most existing LLM agents degrade significantly when human involvement is reduced or made inconsistent, exposing gaps in current agent robustness research. Provides configurable human-participation protocols to stress-test human-AI teaming under realistic conditions.
**Tags:** `agents`, `human-ai-interaction`, `benchmarks`, `evaluation`, `llm-agents`

## [ABot-AgentOS: A General Robotic Agent OS with Lifelong Multi-modal Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10350)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2607.10350)
**TL;DR:** Presents AgentOS, an operating-system-level framework for embodied agents that supports lifelong multi-modal memory, reasoning, tool use, verification, and cross-embodiment execution. The system enables long-horizon tasks by persisting structured knowledge across episodes while supporting heterogeneous robot platforms. Experiments show substantial improvements in multi-step task success rates compared to memoryless baselines.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `agents`, `robotics`, `memory`, `long-horizon`, `multi-modal`

## [OrchestrXR: A Multi-Agent System for Idea-to-Prototype XR Study Authoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01588)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2607.01588)
**TL;DR:** Presents OrchestrXR, a multi-agent pipeline that assists researchers in authoring XR (extended reality) studies from high-level ideas to functional prototypes. User studies show significant time savings and improved prototype quality versus manual authoring.

## [Cortex: A Bidirectionally Aligned Embodied Agent Framework for Long-horizon Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05377)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2607.05377)
**TL;DR:** Proposes Cortex, a framework for robotic manipulation that uses bidirectional alignment between high-level language reasoning and low-level motor control to handle long-horizon tasks. The bidirectional feedback loop allows the agent to recover from failures and replan mid-execution without full task restarts. Evaluation on complex table-top manipulation benchmarks shows state-of-the-art performance on multi-step tasks.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `manipulation`, `long-horizon`, `language-grounding`, `planning`

## [Qwen-VLA: Unifying Vision-Language-Action Modeling across Tasks, Environments, and Robot Embodiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30280)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2605.30280)
**TL;DR:** Introduces Qwen-VLA, a unified embodied foundation model that extends Qwen's vision-language stack to continuous action generation via a diffusion-transformer (DiT)-based action decoder, enabling generalization across diverse robot embodiments and environments. The model achieves strong zero-shot transfer across manipulation, navigation, and dexterous tasks without per-task fine-tuning. Represents a significant step toward a single generalist VLA deployable across heterogeneous robot platforms.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `vla`, `vision-language-action`, `robotics`, `foundation-model`, `generalization`

## [Safe Embodied AI for Long-horizon Tasks: A Cross-layer Analysis of Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05660)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2606.05660)
**TL;DR:** Conducts a cross-layer safety analysis of embodied AI systems performing long-horizon robotic manipulation, identifying failure modes at perception, planning, and execution layers. Proposes safety constraints and monitoring strategies at each layer to reduce the risk of cascading failures during extended task execution. The work highlights that safety must be co-designed across all abstraction layers rather than applied post-hoc.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `safety`, `manipulation`, `long-horizon`, `risk-analysis`

## [A Systematic Approach to Multi-Agent AI from Advanced Regulatory Control Theory: Safe and Auditable LLM Operator Agents for Process Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30877)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2606.30877)
**TL;DR:** Applies advanced control theory to multi-agent LLM systems deployed in industrial process control, framing agent coordination as a regulatory control problem with formal safety and auditability requirements. Demonstrates the approach on chemical process simulations with formal guarantees on bounded deviation.

## [Beyond Individual Intelligence: Surveying Collaboration, Failure Attribution, and Self-Evolution in LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14892)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2605.14892)
**TL;DR:** Provides a comprehensive survey of LLM-based multi-agent systems focusing on three under-studied dimensions: inter-agent collaboration mechanisms, failure attribution methods, and self-evolving agent designs that improve over time. The survey reveals that most current systems lack principled failure diagnosis, leading to brittle performance in dynamic environments. Identifies self-evolution—agents that update their own strategies through reflection—as a key frontier for future research.
**Tags:** `multi-agent`, `agents`, `llm-agents`, `collaboration`, `self-improvement`, `survey`
