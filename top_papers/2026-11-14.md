# Top Papers — AI Agents & Embodied AI (2026-11-14)

## [Rethinking Access Control for AI Agents: Recent Advances and Open Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06764)
**Authors:** (survey paper, multiple contributors)
**TL;DR:** A comprehensive survey of agent access control spanning four areas: policy specification and derivation, identity and delegation, action and resource enforcement, and information flow control. Argues that conventional binary allow/deny models must be replaced with dynamic, context-aware governance as AI agents become deeply integrated into critical systems.
**Tags:** `agents`, `security`, `access-control`, `llm-agents`

**Authors:** (multiple authors)

**Tags:** `agents`, `skill-transfer`, `llm-agents`, `generalization`

## [When Should Agents Remember? Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution for LLM Agents](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/154558)
**Authors:** (NeurIPS 2026 paper)
**TL;DR:** Proposes FGSE (Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution), a framework where each candidate lesson is encoded as a structured hypothesis with explicit precondition, expected behavior change, and an auto-generated verifier. Agents only commit a lesson to memory when it survives active falsification attempts, dramatically improving reliability of self-improvement loops.
**Tags:** `agents`, `self-evolution`, `memory`, `LLM`, `NeurIPS-2026`

---

## [EvoBelief: Falsifiable World Models for Self-Improving LLM Agents](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159510)
**Authors:** (NeurIPS 2026 paper)
**TL;DR:** Introduces EvoBelief, a framework equipping LLM agents with falsifiable world models that evolve through Popperian scientific reasoning—each belief is treated as a testable hypothesis. Agents actively seek disconfirming evidence and update beliefs, achieving more robust task performance compared to agents with fixed world assumptions.
**Tags:** `agents`, `world-models`, `self-improvement`, `NeurIPS-2026`, `falsifiability`

## [GenEA: Generative Agent Representation for Long-Horizon Embodied Plans](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/new)
**Authors:** (multiple authors)
**TL;DR:** Proposes GenEA, a novel generative representation that encodes multi-step, long-horizon embodied plans as structured generative programs. The representation enables an agent to reason over plan structure, adapt to failures mid-execution, and compose sub-plans, outperforming flat action-sequence baselines on complex household tasks.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `planning`, `long-horizon`, `agents`, `representation-learning`

## [Does Your World Model Know Actions Have Consequences?](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.RO/new)
**Authors:** (multiple authors)
**TL;DR:** Examines whether world models in robotic systems properly anticipate downstream causal consequences of actions, revealing systematic failures in handling irreversibility and cascading effects. Introduces a diagnostic benchmark and proposes a consequence-aware training objective that improves planning safety in manipulation and navigation tasks.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `world-models`, `robotics`, `causal-reasoning`, `planning`

## [AFTER: World Model with Adaptive Fast–Slow Timescales for Robot Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.RO/new)
**Authors:** (multiple authors)
**TL;DR:** Introduces AFTER, a world model that maintains separate fast and slow latent streams to handle different rates of change in robot dynamics—fast streams capture immediate physical state while slow streams encode persistent context. Significantly improves generalization to unseen robot configurations and disturbances without retraining.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `world-models`, `robotics`, `temporal-abstraction`, `adaptation`
