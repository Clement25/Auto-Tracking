# Top Papers — AI Agents & Embodied AI (2026-09-10)

## [Embodied AI: From LLMs to World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20021)
**Authors:** (Survey authors, arXiv 2509.20021)
**TL;DR:** A comprehensive survey covering the history, key technologies, and hardware systems of embodied AI, with focus on two paradigms: LLM/MLLM-driven embodied cognition and world model-driven physical interaction. The paper examines how LLMs enable semantic reasoning and task decomposition while world models build internal representations for physics-compliant robot behavior. Applications span robotics, UAVs, and industrial automation, with future directions in lifelong learning and explainability.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `world-models`, `llm`, `robotics`, `survey`

## [Generative World Models of Tasks: LLM-Driven Hierarchical Scaffolding for Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04731)
**Authors:** (arXiv 2509.04731)
**TL;DR:** Proposes a framework where LLMs generate hierarchical task scaffolding for embodied agents by constructing generative world models of tasks. The approach enables agents to plan and execute complex multi-step physical tasks through structured decomposition and execution grounding. Demonstrates improved performance on long-horizon embodied manipulation benchmarks.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `agents`, `world-models`, `llm`, `planning`

## [AgentArch: A Comprehensive Benchmark to Evaluate Agent Architectures in Enterprise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10769)
**Authors:** (arXiv 2509.10769)
**TL;DR:** The first benchmark systematically evaluating 18 agentic architectures across six large language models on realistic enterprise workflows. Analyzes orchestration, prompting style, memory design, and thinking tool integration, revealing significant model-specific architectural preferences that challenge the one-size-fits-all paradigm. Best models achieved only 35.3% success on complex tasks, highlighting large gaps remaining in enterprise-grade AI agents.
**Tags:** `agents`, `benchmark`, `evaluation`, `enterprise`, `llm`

## [The Evolution of Tool Use in LLM Agents: From Single-Tool Call to Multi-Tool Orchestration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22862)
**Authors:** (arXiv 2603.22862)
**TL;DR:** Traces the evolution of tool-using capabilities in LLM agents from early single-step function calls to complex multi-tool orchestration pipelines. Surveys benchmarks, training techniques, and architectural patterns that enable reliable agentic tool use. Proposes a taxonomy of tool-use complexity and identifies unresolved challenges in error recovery and tool composition.
**Tags:** `agents`, `tool-use`, `llm`, `orchestration`, `survey`

## [Understanding Multi-Agent LLM Frameworks: A Unified Benchmark and Experimental Analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03128)
**Authors:** (arXiv 2602.03128)
**TL;DR:** Introduces a unified benchmark for comparing popular multi-agent LLM frameworks (AutoGen, CrewAI, LangGraph, etc.) on standardized task suites. Experimental analysis reveals significant variance across frameworks even with identical underlying models, attributing differences to prompting strategies and agent topology. Provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing multi-agent architectures.
**Tags:** `multi-agent`, `llm`, `benchmark`, `framework`, `evaluation`

## [Agentic Tool Use in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00835)
**Authors:** (arXiv 2604.00835)
**TL;DR:** Systematic study of agentic tool use patterns in large language models, analyzing when and how models decide to invoke external tools versus rely on internal knowledge. Identifies failure modes including over-reliance on tools, hallucinated API calls, and cascading errors in tool chains. Proposes training and prompting interventions to improve tool-use reliability in deployed agent systems.
**Tags:** `agents`, `tool-use`, `llm`, `reliability`, `evaluation`

## [An Empirical Study of Testing Practices in Open Source AI Agent Frameworks and Agentic Applications](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19185v2)
**Authors:** (arXiv 2509.19185)
**TL;DR:** Empirical analysis of testing practices across major open-source AI agent frameworks, revealing that most projects rely on standardized benchmarks rather than unit or integration tests. Identifies coverage gaps in error handling, multi-turn reasoning, and tool-failure scenarios. Calls for the community to adopt software engineering best practices adapted to the non-deterministic nature of LLM-based agents.
