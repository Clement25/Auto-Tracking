**TL;DR:** A benchmark that tests whether tool-using LLM agents can avoid prohibited actions when tool outputs are technically true but omit safety-relevant facts. ## [Same Luck, Different Choice: Twin-World Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31544v1)
**Authors:** Zeropy Surio, Tobias Engel, Mei-Lin Chou et al.
**TL;DR:** Introduces a counterfactual credit assignment method that branches rollouts with shared sampling noise (twin worlds), enabling unbiased estimation of individual decisions' credit within full agentic episodes. This approach addresses a core challenge in RL-based agent training where it is hard to tell which step caused success or failure in a long trajectory.
**Tags:** `agents`, `reinforcement-learning`, `credit-assignment`, `counterfactual`

## [Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31670v1)
**Authors:** Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan et al.
**TL;DR:** Proposes an offline metric based on cross-modal vision-language alignment as a cheap predictor of embodied policy success, avoiding the need for costly closed-loop rollouts during evaluation. The metric serves as a "platonic proxy" — a model-agnostic measure that correlates with real-world performance across diverse robotic foundation models.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `evaluation`, `vision-language`, `robotics`, `metrics`

## [METTA: Memory-Enhanced Test-Time Adaptation for Meta-Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31656v1)
**Authors:** Ali Najar, Hosein Hasani, Amir Kooshan Fattah et al.
**TL;DR:** A memory-based framework for test-time adaptation in meta-RL that maintains structured task evidence over long interaction histories, enabling faster adaptation to unseen tasks than recurrent or transformer baselines. METTA's explicit memory allows agents to quickly infer task identity and adjust their policies without additional gradient updates.
**Tags:** `agents`, `meta-reinforcement-learning`, `memory`, `adaptation`, `test-time`
