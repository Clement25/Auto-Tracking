# Top Papers — AI Agents & Embodied AI (2026-09-08)

## [Repo-To-Skill: Distilling GitHub Repositories Into AI4AI Skills](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02749)
**Authors:** VectorSpaceLab Team (Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence)
**TL;DR:** Introduces DisCo, a skill-powered research agent that automatically extracts "operational knowledge" from 1,000+ GitHub repositories into 5,000+ verified, reusable ML skills. Equipped with this AREX-Skill Library, a GPT-5.5-backed research agent scores 134.3% higher on MLE-bench and 34.4% higher on PaperBench vs. the same agent without skills—demonstrating that distilled repo-know-how is the missing layer in autonomous research agents.
**Tags:** `agents`, `research-agents`, `skill-distillation`, `automated-ml`, `knowledge-extraction`

## [Terminal-Universe: Turning Agent Trajectories into Scalable Terminal Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04148)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2609.04148, submitted Sept 4, 2026)
**TL;DR:** Terminal-Universe reconstructs code-agent training workspaces by replaying file operations logged in prior agent trajectories, producing 37,300 task-sufficient terminal environments from public traces. A completion agent fills in missing files, then synthesizes cross-codebase queries and multi-round user sessions, improving Qwen3.5-27B single-round performance on Terminal-Bench 2.1 by 11.9 points and multi-round performance on EvoCode-Bench v2 by 13.8 points.
**Tags:** `agents`, `training-data`, `terminal-agents`, `code-agents`, `environment-synthesis`

## [Skill-DisCo: Distilling and Compiling Agent Traces into Reusable Procedural Skills](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26669)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2606.26669)
**TL;DR:** Skill-DisCo proposes a framework for extracting reusable procedural skills from recorded agent execution traces, enabling agents to accumulate know-how across tasks rather than re-deriving solutions from scratch each run. The distilled skills are compiled into compact, verifiable modules that plug directly into downstream agent harnesses.
**Tags:** `agents`, `skill-learning`, `procedural-knowledge`, `agent-memory`, `reuse`

## [Safe Embodied AI for Long-horizon Tasks: A Cross-layer Analysis of Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05660)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2606.05660, June 2026)
**TL;DR:** Analyzes safety challenges in embodied AI systems performing long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks, conducting a cross-layer study spanning perception, planning, and actuation. The paper proposes a taxonomy of failure modes and safety constraints, providing a framework for building more reliable manipulation agents in real-world deployments.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `manipulation`, `safety`, `long-horizon`

## [SYMPHONY: Synergistic Multi-agent Planning with Heterogeneous Language Model Assembly](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09171)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2506.09171)
**TL;DR:** SYMPHONY assembles a diverse pool of heterogeneous LLM agents within a Monte Carlo Tree Search planning framework to increase rollout diversity and improve multi-step reasoning quality. By routing subtasks to specialized models based on capability profiles, it achieves better performance on complex planning benchmarks than homogeneous single-model baselines.
**Tags:** `agents`, `multi-agent`, `planning`, `MCTS`, `LLM-heterogeneous`

## [CAR-bench: Evaluating Consistency and Limit-Awareness of LLM Agents under Real-World Uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/current)
**Authors:** (Recent submission, cs.AI)
**TL;DR:** Introduces CAR-bench, a benchmark that tests LLM agent behavior in multi-turn, tool-using scenarios with injected uncertainty and ambiguity. It specifically probes for consistency across rephrased queries and for whether agents correctly recognize the limits of their own capabilities, revealing significant failure modes in current frontier models.
**Tags:** `agents`, `benchmarking`, `evaluation`, `uncertainty`, `tool-use`

## [Multi-agent Embodied AI: Advances and Future Directions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05108)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2505.05108, May 2026)
**TL;DR:** A comprehensive survey of multi-agent embodied AI systems covering cooperative navigation, object rearrangement, task delegation, and communication protocols among physically situated agents. The paper identifies sim-to-real transfer and scalable coordination as the two biggest open challenges, and outlines a research roadmap toward deployable multi-robot systems.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `multi-agent`, `robotics`, `survey`, `sim-to-real`

## [Embodied AI in Action: Insights from SAE World Congress 2026 on Safety, Trust, Robotics, and Real-World Deployment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10653)
**Authors:** SAE World Congress 2026 Panel (arXiv:2605.10653)
**TL;DR:** A white paper synthesizing insights from an expert panel spanning automotive, robotics, AI, and safety engineering at the 2026 SAE World Congress. Key themes include trust calibration in human-robot teaming, liability frameworks for autonomous embodied agents, and the gap between simulation-validated and field-validated performance.

## [Self-Evolving Embodied AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04411)
**Authors:** Tongtong Feng, Xin Wang, Wenwu Zhu (Zhejiang University, Feb 2026)
**TL;DR:** Proposes a framework for embodied agents that continuously update their own policies through interaction with open-ended environments—combining self-generated curricula, intrinsic motivation, and model-based rollout planning. Experiments in simulated household and outdoor navigation tasks show progressive skill acquisition without human-labeled reward engineering.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `self-improving`, `curriculum-learning`, `robotics`, `open-ended`

## [Why Reasoning Fails to Plan: A Planning-Centric Analysis of Long-Horizon Decision Making in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22311)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2601.22311, Jan 2026)
**TL;DR:** Provides a rigorous analysis of where chain-of-thought reasoning breaks down in long-horizon planning tasks for LLM agents, identifying specific failure modes including state-tracking drift, goal abandonment, and backtracking deficits. The paper proposes structured planning primitives that significantly reduce error accumulation in multi-step decision-making benchmarks.
**Tags:** `agents`, `planning`, `reasoning`, `LLM`, `long-horizon`
