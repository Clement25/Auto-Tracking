# Top Papers — AI Agents & Embodied AI (2026-10-30)

## [Precedent or Intent? What Coding Agents Follow When Evolving Existing Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38244)
**Authors:** Haichao Zhu, et al.
**TL;DR:** Investigates whether coding agents prioritize legacy implementation patterns or explicit new task instructions when modifying an existing codebase. The study finds that agents often defer to precedent even when instructions contradict it, revealing a key tension in agentic software engineering. Results have direct implications for how humans should spec change requests to code agents.
**Tags:** `agents`, `coding-agents`, `LLM`, `software-engineering`

---

## [Is it my preference? Role Grounding Attack against Agent's Memory via Social Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38209)
**Authors:** Haoran Bu, et al.
**TL;DR:** Presents a novel attack where malicious actors manipulate agent memory through social platforms, causing the agent to misattribute injected preferences as its own. The attack exploits the way agents ground roles and personal context, making it a persistent and stealthy threat. Defenses based on memory provenance tracking are proposed.
**Tags:** `agents`, `security`, `adversarial`, `memory`, `LLM`

---

## [Disparity Laundering: Consensus Without Fairness in Clinical Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38215)
**Authors:** Xiaoyang Wang, et al.
**TL;DR:** Demonstrates that deliberative consensus among clinical LLM agents does not reduce demographic bias, and can actually amplify disparities across different model families and topologies. This challenges the assumption that multi-agent deliberation improves fairness. The paper provides an audit framework for evaluating bias in clinical multi-agent pipelines.
**Tags:** `multi-agent`, `fairness`, `clinical-AI`, `LLM`, `bias`

---

## [Skeleton Coding: Program Structure as a Specification for Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38214)
**Authors:** Aditya Golatkar, et al.
**TL;DR:** Proposes using program skeletons—structural outlines of code—as lightweight behavioral specifications that reduce the burden on developers to write explicit contracts. Coding agents conditioned on skeletons show improved adherence to intended design. The approach bridges the gap between formal spec and informal instruction.
**Tags:** `agents`, `coding-agents`, `program-synthesis`, `specification`

---

## [DecompGym: Benchmarking LLM Agents on Designing Decomposition Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38175)
**Authors:** Sikai Cheng, et al.
**TL;DR:** A new benchmark that tests whether LLM agents can autonomously design Benders decomposition and column generation algorithms for combinatorial optimization problems. The benchmark reveals significant gaps between current frontier models and expert human operations researchers. Includes 50 problem instances across five domains.
**Tags:** `agents`, `benchmark`, `LLM`, `optimization`, `mathematical-reasoning`

## [CuraEval: Evaluating Demonstration Curation on Mixed-Skill Robot Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38221)
**Authors:** Jiuyi Xu, et al.
**TL;DR:** Evaluates diverse methods for selecting imitation-learning demonstrations from teleoperation datasets where operator skill levels vary widely. Finds that naive filtering by success rate underperforms more nuanced quality metrics, especially in multi-task settings. Provides an open benchmark to standardize demonstration curation research.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `imitation-learning`, `data-curation`

---

## [Embodied at Sea: Imitation Learning for Multi-Task Underwater Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38199)
**Authors:** Greta Perez-Haiek, et al.
**TL;DR:** Addresses the challenge of behavior cloning in underwater robotic manipulation, where visual distortion, currents, and buoyancy create distribution shift absent from lab training data. Proposes domain-aware data augmentation and policy regularization techniques. Results show robust transfer across simulated and real underwater conditions.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `underwater`, `imitation-learning`, `manipulation`

## [POD-RoPE: Rotary Position Encoding over Physical Observation Distributions for Embodied AI](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=POD-RoPE+embodied+AI&searchtype=all)
**Authors:** (from DailyArXiv digest, 2026-10-30)
**TL;DR:** Proposes a novel positional encoding scheme that conditions rotary position embeddings on the statistical distribution of physical observations in an agent's history. This improves temporal coherence in long-horizon embodied tasks compared to standard sequence-length-based RoPE. Validated across navigation and manipulation benchmarks.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `position-encoding`, `transformer`, `robotics`

## [Two Birds with One Stone: Skill-Augmented Generation via Unified Retrieval and Utilization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38204)
**Authors:** Zihan Luo, et al.
**TL;DR:** A single model architecture that jointly handles skill retrieval and skill utilization, avoiding the pipeline failures common in modular retrieve-then-use systems. By training the two functions together, the model learns to fetch only skills that are actually usable in context. Outperforms retrieval-augmented baselines on multi-step reasoning benchmarks.
**Tags:** `agents`, `skill-learning`, `retrieval-augmented`, `LLM`
