# Top Papers — AI Agents & Embodied AI (2026-09-12)

## [An Exploratory Study of Agent Plans for Agentic AI Coding Tools in Open-Source Software](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04661)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2608.04661, August 2026)
**TL;DR:** Examines "Agent Plans" — how a task is framed before or during execution, including intended changes, relevant files, and completion checks — across agentic AI coding tools in open-source software. Reveals patterns in how coding agents decompose, locate, and verify changes that can inform better tool design. Provides empirical evidence of what makes agent planning effective in real-world repositories.
**Tags:** `agents`, `coding-agents`, `software-engineering`, `planning`, `tool-use`

## [Agentic Tool Use in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00835)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2604.00835, April 2026)
**TL;DR:** A comprehensive survey organizing the LLM-as-agent literature into three paradigms: prompting as plug-and-play, supervised tool learning, and reward-driven tool policy learning. Analyzes methods, strengths, and failure modes across each paradigm, providing a unified framework for understanding how LLMs are deployed as autonomous agents. Identifies key gaps and future directions in tool-augmented language modeling.
**Tags:** `agents`, `tool-use`, `LLM`, `survey`, `reinforcement-learning`

---

## [Efficient Agentic Reasoning Through Self-Regulated Simulative Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22138)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2605.22138, May 2026)
**TL;DR:** Introduces a self-regulated simulative planning framework that enables agents to mentally simulate potential action trajectories before committing, reducing costly trial-and-error in multi-step reasoning tasks. The approach dynamically adjusts planning depth based on task complexity, achieving significant efficiency gains. Evaluated across agentic benchmarks, it consistently outperforms reactive baselines with fewer environment interactions.
**Tags:** `agents`, `planning`, `reasoning`, `efficiency`, `simulation`

---

## [Tools as Continuous Flow for Evolving Agentic Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07339)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2605.07339, May 2026)
**TL;DR:** Proposes treating tool invocations as continuous flows within agentic pipelines rather than discrete function calls, enabling smoother mid-reasoning tool use and dynamic tool chaining. The framework allows agents to evolve their reasoning strategies as new tool outputs arrive, without re-starting inference. Demonstrates improved performance on complex compositional tasks requiring interleaved reasoning and action.
**Tags:** `agents`, `tool-use`, `reasoning`, `LLM`, `architecture`

## [Understanding Multi-Agent LLM Frameworks: A Unified Benchmark and Experimental Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03128)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2602.03128, February 2026)
**TL;DR:** Presents a unified benchmark comparing major multi-agent LLM frameworks (AutoGen, CrewAI, LangGraph, etc.) across task categories including coding, reasoning, and planning. Experimental analysis reveals significant variance in framework performance depending on task structure and communication topology. Provides actionable guidance for selecting or designing multi-agent architectures for specific use cases.
**Tags:** `multi-agent`, `LLM`, `benchmark`, `evaluation`, `framework`

## [From Chatbot to Digital Colleague: The Paradigm Shift Toward Persistent Autonomous AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14502)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2606.14502, June 2026)
**TL;DR:** Argues that AI systems are transitioning from stateless question-answering chatbots to persistent autonomous agents that maintain goals, memory, and ongoing tasks over time. The paper characterizes the architectural and behavioral properties required for this shift, including persistent state, proactive task initiation, and inter-agent delegation. Discusses implications for alignment, oversight, and human-AI collaboration.
**Tags:** `agents`, `autonomy`, `persistent-AI`, `alignment`, `human-AI-interaction`

## [Embodied AI in Action: Insights from SAE World Congress 2026 on Safety, Trust, Robotics, and Real-World Deployment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10653)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2605.10653, May 2026)
**TL;DR:** Synthesizes findings from SAE World Congress 2026 where automotive, robotics, and AI safety experts discussed the rapid move of embodied AI into the physical world. Highlights key challenges: ensuring safety and reliability in unstructured environments, building human trust in autonomous systems, and governance frameworks for real-world deployment. Identifies sim-to-real gap, edge-case handling, and certification as critical open problems.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `safety`, `deployment`, `autonomous-vehicles`

## [Foundation Models in Robotics: A Comprehensive Review of Methods, Models, Datasets, Challenges and Future Research Directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15395)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2604.15395, April 2026)
**TL;DR:** Provides an extensive review of how foundation models — LLMs, VLMs, and multimodal models — are being adapted for robotic perception, planning, and control. Catalogs major models, training datasets, and evaluation benchmarks across manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction tasks. Identifies transfer learning, data scarcity, and real-time inference as key open challenges.

## [Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA: From Vision-Language-Action Models to a Real-World Robot Learning Stack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14409)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2606.14409, June 2026)
**TL;DR:** Presents a full robot learning stack built on a vision-language-action (VLA) model, covering perception, instruction following, and motor control for real-world manipulation tasks. The 0.5B-parameter model achieves competitive performance on standard robot benchmarks while being deployable on edge hardware. Demonstrates end-to-end learning from human demonstrations with minimal environment-specific tuning.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `VLA`, `vision-language-action`, `manipulation`

## [Embodied AI with Foundation Models for Mobile Service Robots: A Systematic Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20503)
**Authors:** (arXiv:2505.20503, May 2026)
**TL;DR:** Systematically reviews the use of LLMs, VLMs, and multimodal foundation models for mobile service robots that must navigate, communicate, and assist in human environments. Covers scene understanding, task planning, natural language grounding, and safe navigation. Concludes that integration of foundation model reasoning with real-time sensorimotor control remains the central unsolved challenge.
**Tags:** `embodied-ai`, `robotics`, `mobile-robots`, `foundation-models`, `survey`

## [SWE-2: Pushing the Pareto Frontier of Coding Agents](https://paperswithcode.co/paper/114152)
**Authors:** Cognition AI (September 10, 2026)
**TL;DR:** Introduces SWE-2, a coding agent post-trained from Kimi K3 (2.8T parameters) using reinforcement learning scaled to the multi-trillion-parameter regime for the first time. Introduces variable reasoning effort levels (medium, high, max) trained in a single RL run rather than separate models. Claims performance within 1 point of top frontier models at up to 70% lower cost.
**Tags:** `agents`, `coding-agents`, `reinforcement-learning`, `SWE-bench`, `LLM`
