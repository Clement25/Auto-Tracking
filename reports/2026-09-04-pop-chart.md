## Highlights

### US
Ella Langley's country-crossover smash "Choosin' Texas" reaches its historic 20th consecutive week at #1 on the Billboard Hot 100, surpassing all non-holiday songs in chart history — only Mariah Carey's perennial "All I Want for Christmas Is You" (22 weeks across multiple holiday seasons) stands above it. The week of September 5 also sees strong country representation with Morgan Wallen's "Been By Now" at #4, while pop royalty asserts itself: Taylor Swift's "I Knew It, I Knew You" holds firm at #3, and Ariana Grande's "Hate That I Made You Love Me" — the lead single from her eagerly anticipated album *Petal* — anchors the top 5 at #5 after previously topping the chart itself. The cross-genre mix of country, pop, and K-pop (Tame Impala & JENNIE's "Dracula" sits just outside the top 5) signals an unusually diverse chart landscape for early fall.

### EU
The UK chart is dominated by Sam Fender & Olivia Dean's emotional indie-folk duet "Rein Me In," which has broken the 73-year-old record to become the longest-running #1 single in UK Official Charts history. The song — a raw, soaring anthem about co-dependency and letting go — was also crowned the UK's Official Song of the Summer 2026. Dance music remains strong with HUGEL's sun-drenched house track "Movin' To The Sun" feat. Imael Angel & Ultra Naté firmly at #2. The transatlantic crossover continues, with US country hits "Choosin' Texas" and "Boston" occupying spots #3 and #4, while newcomer Sienna Spiro's "Great Expectation" rounds out the top 5.

### CN
The QQ Music 热歌榜 this week is dominated by veteran Mandopop voices. Taiwanese pop icon Stefanie Sun (孙燕姿) holds both #1 ("我不难过") and #5 ("我怀念的"), reflecting a wave of nostalgia-pop sentiment sweeping Chinese streaming platforms. Taiwan-based power-ballad singer 李佳薇 rises to #2 with the heartbreak duet "甲乙丙丁 (你我怎么两清)," while 许茹芸's sentimental classic-era hit "泪海" enjoys a viral resurgence driven by Douyin (TikTok China) short-video clips at #3. Li Ronghao's (李荣浩) smooth R&B love song "恋人" sits comfortably at #4. Overall the chart reflects the enduring dominance of emotional C-pop balladry among streaming audiences on the mainland.
