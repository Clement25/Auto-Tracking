## Highlights

### US
Country music achieved an unprecedented milestone this week: for the first time in Billboard Hot 100 history, country songs occupy all five of the top five positions simultaneously. Ella Langley leads the pack with "Choosin' Texas" in its 16th week at #1 — now the longest-running No. 1 by a solo woman in Hot 100 history (outside Mariah Carey's holiday perennial). Her duet with Morgan Wallen, "I Can't Love You Anymore" from her album *Dandelion*, holds at #4, making Langley the dominant force of 2026. Morgan Wallen's new single "Been by Now" debuted at #2, one of the year's biggest debut positions. Taylor Swift maintains #3 with continued streaming strength.

### EU
The UK chart is living through a historic moment: Sam Fender & Olivia Dean's "Rein Me In" reached its 20th consecutive week at #1, shattering a 73-year-old UK chart record held since 1953 by Frankie Laine's "I Believe." Guinness World Records confirmed the achievement. The song, which began as a TikTok viral phenomenon in mid-2025 and debuted at #86 before slowly climbing, also won Song of the Year at the 2026 BRIT Awards. Meanwhile, Shakira and Burna Boy's "Dai Dai" — the official song of the FIFA World Cup 2026 — has climbed to #2, giving Shakira her first UK top-10 hit in over 15 years. The US country wave also crossed the Atlantic, with Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas" reaching #3 in the UK — a rare feat for a contemporary country act.

### CN
The Chinese charts this week are dominated by emotionally resonant Mandopop ballads and romantic duets. Veteran Taiwanese singer Li Jia Wei (Jess Lee) tops the combined Taiwan-Mainland chart with "甲乙丙丁" (Jia Yi Bing Ding), a powerful breakup ballad about emotional accounting in a relationship that resonated strongly with younger listeners. Jam Hsiao and A-Lin's duet "爱上你不是我决定" debuted in the top 3, generating immediate buzz on Weibo and Douyin. Indie-pop track "玻璃" (Bo Li) by Gareth.T continues to accumulate favorites on NetEase (1.5M+) after its May release, sustained by Douyin short-video usage. The Douyin-driven lyric-share culture remains the key engine for slow-burn chart climbers on Chinese platforms.
