## Highlights

### US

Ella Langley is the undisputed queen of the Hot 100 this week, holding both the #1 and #5 spots simultaneously. "Choosin' Texas" logged its eighth week at the top — the year's longest-running #1 single so far — cementing her crossover country-pop dominance. Bruno Mars continues his commercial resurgence at #2 with "I Just Might," a funk-laced groove that has lingered in the top three for several weeks. Olivia Dean's introspective "Man I Need" climbed to #3, while Olivia Rodrigo keeps "Drop Dead" in the top five with its sharp alt-pop edge. The week underscores a broad genre mix at the top, with country-pop, classic funk revival, and guitar-driven pop all competing neck-and-neck.

### EU

The UK chart tells a story of indie credibility meeting mainstream momentum. Sam Fender and Olivia Dean's "Rein Me In" holds the top spot, a poignant folk-pop duet that ousted Olivia Rodrigo's "Drop Dead" from #1 earlier this month. Tame Impala's "Dracula" is a remarkable chart story: the psych-rock band's atmospheric single climbed to #2, indicating strong crossover appeal beyond their cult following. "Billie Jean" by Michael Jackson at #4 reflects a wave of catalog resurgence tied to renewed biopic interest. Alex Warren's "Fever Dream" rounds out the top 5 with emotional pop-rock that has resonated widely across European streaming platforms.

### CN

The Chinese market this week is split between a buzzy surprise release and an ongoing blockbuster album campaign. Yu Shuxin's (虞书欣) "人间最美好的事" (The Most Fleeting Thing In The World) debuted to extraordinary reception — released on April 21 with no advance promotion, it shot to #1 in under 30 minutes and accumulated over 41,000 listeners on the Global Chinese Music Chart for the week of May 2–8. Jay Chou (周杰伦) continues to dominate with multiple tracks from his 16th studio album *Children of the Sun* (released March 26), which has sold over 100 million RMB. The viral AI-assisted cover of "待我如初" (Treat Me As Before) has also broken through on NetEase Cloud Music and Douyin, reflecting the growing role of AI-generated vocal covers in driving chart activity on Chinese platforms.
